Welcome to BlightCast 🌿

BlightCast runs the USPest.org boxwood blight infection-risk model (Oregon State University, v3.0) on the weather at your spot — the last month of actual conditions plus the 7-day forecast — so you can see accumulated infection risk, set your own alert level, and time protective sprays. Start on the ⚙ Setup tab and add your 📍 location (type your town — that's the whole setup). Your data stays in this browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
📖 How the model works

Boxwood blight (Calonectria pseudonaviculata) infects when leaves are wet and temperatures are mild. This tool implements the boxwood blight infection risk model v3.0 published by Len Coop at Oregon State University for USPest.org — a "degree-hours during leaf wetness" model:

  • Each wet hour between 44°F and 86°F adds infection risk units (degree-hours, DH) from the model's published temperature table — rising to a peak of 31.35 DH/hour at 76°F, then falling to zero at 86°F.
  • Dry hours add nothing, and 6 straight dry hours end the infection cycle — accumulated DH resets to zero.
  • Without a leaf-wetness sensor, BlightCast estimates wetness each hour from Open-Meteo weather: rain (following the model's precipitation-wetness rules) and humidity/dew (wet when RH reaches your dew threshold — default 90% — in light wind). You can tune the sensitivity in ⚙ Setup — or bring in real station data there (📡): link a nearby NEWA network station, connect your own station's cloud service (LI-COR Cloud or any CSV/JSON data feed), or import a CSV by hand. Station readings override Open-Meteo for the hours they cover, and a real leaf-wetness sensor is used by the model directly.

Accumulated DH maps to the model's published risk levels:

Lesion counts come from the model's regressions (highly susceptible = English/true dwarf boxwood, B. sempervirens 'Suffruticosa'; susceptible = common American boxwood, B. sempervirens). Less susceptible cultivars run lower risk than shown — set what grows at each location with the 🌱 dropdown in ⚙ Setup and the guidance lines adjust.

⚠ Model assumptions — read once
  • The model assumes inoculum is present and the host is a susceptible, not-yet-protected cultivar. It predicts conditions favorable for infection — not that infection has happened.
  • Lesion estimates are approximate, for gauging severity risk only.
  • Plants under overhead irrigation or heavy shade run higher risk than the model shows (it assumes ambient exposure).
  • In the very humid Southeast, humidity alone can sometimes wet canopies more than models capture — if outbreaks outrun the numbers, lower the dew threshold in ⚙ Setup (e.g. 87%).
  • A forecast is a forecast: the last 7 days shown are actual weather, the next 7 are the forecast, and numbers can differ from a weather station a few miles away. Scout your plants — the model never replaces monitoring.

📍 Locations
🌱 The boxwood-type dropdown on each location tailors the guidance lines (first-infection level, lesion estimates) to what grows there — the chart bands stay the model's published levels either way. 📡 Real station data (optional — sharpens the model)
BlightCast normally runs on Open-Meteo's modeled weather for your exact spot. Real station readings override Open-Meteo for the hours they cover — and a real leaf-wetness sensor is used by the model directly, which beats estimating wetness from humidity. Open-Meteo still fills any gaps plus the 7-day forecast. Everything here is PER LOCATION — each of your locations can have its own station, its own connection, its own imports (they never share). Here's what each location uses right now; the connect options below act on the active location.
If there's a station near you on NEWA (Cornell's ag-weather network — includes partner networks like Rutgers NJWxNet and many nursery/orchard stations, most with leaf-wetness sensors), BlightCast can pull its hourly data automatically — no file handling, refreshed every time you open the page. Reading station data is free and needs no account.
If your weather station reports to a cloud service, BlightCast can pull from it directly. LI-COR Cloud (HOBO RX loggers) is built in — paste the API token from licor.cloud → Data → API and pick your logger; a leaf-wetness sensor on it is used by the model directly. Any other service works through a data-feed link: an address that returns readings as CSV or JSON (most station services and mesonets have one — the key goes in the address), mapped once on a preview screen and refreshed automatically after that. Tokens and links stay in this browser and are never included in 🔗 setup share-links.
What the file needs: a header row naming the columns, then one row per hour (finer is fine — 15-minute rows are averaged into hours automatically). Required: a date+time column (or separate date and time columns) and air temperature. Used when present: humidity %, rain, wind speed, and leaf wetness (minutes-wet-per-hour, a 0–10 score, or wet/dry — any of the three). Dates are read as month/day/year, times as local time at the station; columns can be in any order and named almost anything — you confirm the mapping on a preview screen before anything is saved. Re-import a fresh download any time: new hours merge in. The file is read right here in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
Known-good sources: Davis WeatherLink exports, Onset HOBO, Spectrum WatchDog, Rainwise, USPest.org hourly downloads, and most state mesonet "download hourly data" pages. If a file won't read, the preview screen says why — usually the date column pick.
💧 Leaf-wetness sensitivity
Leaves count as wet during rain, right after rain, and when the air is humid and calm. These two dials tune the humidity side.
Defaults (90% · 1) match the model's own settings. Humid Southeast: try 87%. Getting wet-hours on days you know were dry: raise the dew threshold, or require more wetness (2–3).
🧴 My products
Your usual protective sprays — name, how many days the label says it protects, and (optionally) the FRAC group. They become one-click presets in the 🧴 Log-a-spray form, and a FRAC group turns on a gentle rotation nudge (warning only — it never blocks a save).
Wash-off: rain since application reaching this total ENDS the protection early (label days always stay the ceiling — a dry spell never stretches them). Common protectant guidance is around 1″.
🔔 Alerts on this computer
When ON, BlightCast chimes and pops a note whenever a new forecast crossing of your alert level appears — it works while the page is open. For reminders that reach you when BlightCast is closed, use the 📅 button on the re-spray recommendation: the calendar file carries its own 12-hour and 1-hour reminders into Outlook, Gmail or Apple Calendar.
📤 Spray log → a shared Google Sheet
Give the whole team a live list of what's been applied without anyone opening BlightCast: every 🧴 log entry (and removals) is written to a Google Sheet you own. One-time setup, about 3 minutes:
  1. Make a Google Form with short-answer questions: Date, Product, Days, FRAC, Location, Notes, Status, DH (name them however you like; Date-type questions work too). Leave every question NOT-required, set Settings → Responses → Collect email addresses: OFF — and then click Publish (top right; an unpublished form rejects every row).
  2. In the form's Responses tab click the Sheets icon — that spreadsheet is the shared log.
  3. In the form: ⋮ menu → Get pre-filled link → type the words date, product, days, frac, location, notes, status, dh into their matching boxes → Get link → copy → paste below.
Sends are one-way and invisible (Google doesn't let the page read the result) — the 🧪 test row confirms the pipe once. The connection rides your backup/share file, so a coworker who imports it writes to the same sheet.

ℹ About BlightCast

Privacy, plainly: there is no account, no install, and nothing you enter is uploaded anywhere. Your locations, settings, spray log and any weather file you import are stored only in this browser on this computer (imported files are read right here in the browser). The only internet requests are to Open-Meteo (a free public weather service) for hourly weather, town search and the last-year archive — plus, only for sources you connect yourself in ⚙ Setup: NEWA's public station service at Cornell (newa.nrcc.cornell.edu / hrly.nrcc.cornell.edu), LI-COR Cloud (api.licor.cloud — your token goes only there, stored only in this browser), a data-feed address you provide, or — if you connect the optional 📤 sheet bridge — spray-log rows written to the Google Form/Sheet you set up. Tokens and feed links are never included in 🔗 setup share-links.

A model is a model: BlightCast is planning help, not a diagnosis. It flags conditions favorable for infection assuming the pathogen is around — scouting and the product label always win. See the 📖 Model tab for assumptions and credits.
