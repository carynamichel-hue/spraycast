🌦 SprayCast spray timing & planning

🌦 What SprayCast is

SprayCast helps you plan your spraying around the weather. You tell it your limits — how much wind you'll accept, what gust stops you, how much rain chance is too much, which hours of the week you're willing to spray — and it colours the next two weeks of forecast against them, hour by hour, so the hours that suit you are obvious at a glance.

It is free, has no account, and there is no SprayCast server. Everything you enter lives in the browser you enter it in.

What it is not: a spray record, a chemical database, or a compliance system. It is about timing only. The product label always wins, and conditions in the yard beat any model — check them before you pull out.

1 · Two minutes to running

  1. Open ⚙ Setup and search for your town under 📍 Locations. Search by town name — "Bridgeton NJ" works, a street address does not.
  2. Go to 📆 Planner. You now have 14 days of forecast, rated against a starting set of limits.
  3. Back in Setup, open ⚙ Condition profiles → ✎ Manage profiles and make the limits yours. This is the step that makes the rest of the app worth anything — the colours mean nothing until they mean your rules.

More than one block or site? Add each as its own location. Two sites in the same town? Save the town twice and give each a ✎ nickname — each keeps its own forecast point, map view, scheduled sprays and station.

2 · Condition profiles — your definition of a good hour

A profile holds the limits an hour is judged against:

  • Wind fine below / stops you at — under the first number the hour is good, between them it's marginal, above the second it's blocked.
  • Gusts stop you at — usually the number that actually decides the day.
  • Rain chance and temperature range — same two-step idea.
  • Wind FROM — tick only the directions you'll accept if there's something downwind you must not hit. Leave all unticked for any direction.
  • Spray hours — per weekday. Set the end EARLIER than the start for an overnight window: Tuesday 20:00 → 04:00 means Tuesday evening into Wednesday morning, and SprayCast treats it as one continuous run.

Times snap to whole hours when you save — starts round up and ends round down, so a half-hour you excluded is never offered back to you.

Keep more than one profile if different jobs have different rules — a tight drift-sensitive airblast profile and a relaxed one for a soil drench, say — and switch between them in the white bar. The colours redraw instantly.

3 · Reading the 📆 Planner

  • The day cards — 14 days at a glance: "Good · 9h", "Tight · 2h" or "No window", plus the best stretch. On a phone they swipe sideways. Tap one for its hour-by-hour table.
  • 📆 Week planner — 24 hours down, 7 days across. Green is good, amber marginal, red blocked by weather, grey outside your spray hours (striped grey means the weather would have worked but you don't spray then). Hover or tap any cell for the reason.
  • ◀ ▶ — forward through the forecast, or back about 13 weeks through the weather that actually happened, with your sprays drawn where you scheduled them. Useful in an argument about what last Tuesday was really like.
  • ⛶ — the same week full screen, turned sideways so it fits a phone held upright. 📸 saves it as a picture to text to someone.
  • 👀 Other farms — overlay another location's scheduled sprays, for when the same crew or the same sprayer has to cover both.
  • The hour table — every hour with its reason. An hour outside your spray hours still tells you what the weather was doing, so you can decide whether to widen the window rather than assume the weather was against you.

4 · Scheduling a spray

📌 Plan Spray in the white bar opens a drawer already filled in with that day's best window. Name the spray, adjust the hours, save.

  • It draws on the week grid and appears in 📌 Sprays.
  • 📅 Calendar downloads a calendar file with 12-hour and 1-hour reminders built in — open it and the spray lands in Outlook, Google or Apple Calendar. This is the only reminder that reaches you when SprayCast is closed.
  • ⚠ Forecast shifted — if the weather moves under a spray you've already scheduled, its block gets a dashed red outline and the ledger says how many of its hours are now outside limits. It is re-checked against the profile the spray was committed under, not whichever one you have selected now.
  • Mark it ✓ Done when it's sprayed. A dated spray that passed without being marked done says "past" in red rather than sitting there looking planned.

5 · ⏰ Watches — let it tell you

A watch is a standing question: "when does a 4-hour calm window with NW wind next turn up for block A?" SprayCast checks every forecast day and shows the next match — across every location the watch names, each judged on its own forecast.

Matched windows are pencilled onto the week grid as dashed amber outlines. 📌 Schedule turns a match into a real scheduled spray in one click (switching farms first if the match is at another one). A one-time watch is used up by scheduling it; tick Recurring for something you do every week.

6 · 🧭 The wind map

Satellite imagery of your ground with the wind drawn over it. The big yellow arrow points where spray would DRIFT — downwind — not where the wind comes from. The cone behind it is the rough drift fan. Small white arrows show the wind across the whole view: where they disagree with each other, the ground is turning the wind.

  • Drag to pan, pinch or ＋／－ to zoom. Each location remembers its own view.
  • The slider walks 48 hours ahead — watch the arrow swing before committing to a day.
  • 📍 Use this map center re-anchors the forecast point on your actual ground rather than the middle of town. Worth doing once per site.
  • 🎛 Set wind manually answers "which wind direction is safe for this block?" before the forecast offers one.

7 · 🌐 Better weather, and 📋 how good the forecast has been

Out of the box every number comes from Open-Meteo's model of the grid square your farm sits in. Two things can improve on that, both in ⚙ Setup:

  • A station — a LI-COR/HOBO logger of your own, a nearby NEWA station (free, mostly Northeast and Midwest), or a CSV your station exported. A station cannot forecast, so it never touches future hours; what it does is replace the ◀ look-back with what actually happened on your ground.
  • 🇺🇸 The National Weather Service forecast — a second opinion for the hours ahead. It usually agrees with Open-Meteo on temperature and wind speed and disagrees on wind direction, which is the number drift depends on.

That is what the 📋 Forecast scorecard is for. It compares what the forecast said against what was measured — at your own station if you have one, otherwise the nearest National Weather Service station, and it always says which and how far away. It leads with wind direction because that is the number that decides where the spray goes.

It needs a couple of weeks spanning different weather before it means much, and it says so rather than showing a confident number built from a handful of hours. When it has enough: if direction scores loose at your farm, leave a wider buffer downwind. If gusts score loose, keep your gust limit further from the forecast number.

8 · 📤 Sharing with the crew (the Google-Sheet bridge)

Optional, and worth three minutes: scheduled sprays get written to a Google Sheet you own, so the crew or the boss can see what's planned without opening SprayCast. Done and cancelled updates follow.

  1. Make a Google Form where every question is short-answer — including the date one: Date, Start, End, Spray, Location, Notes, Status.
  2. Leave every question NOT required. Settings → Responses → Collect email addresses: OFF and Limit to 1 response: OFF — either one makes Google demand a sign-in, and background rows can't sign in.
  3. Publish the form (top right). This is the one everybody misses — an unpublished form rejects every row.
  4. In the form's Responses tab, click the Sheets icon. That spreadsheet is your shared list.
  5. ⋮ menu → Get pre-filled link → type the words date, start, end, spray, location, notes, status into their matching boxes → press Get link at the BOTTOM of the page → Copy link → paste it into SprayCast.

Don't copy the address bar — it carries none of the words you typed, which is the other thing everybody hits.

Rows land on the sheet's "Form Responses" tab, not Sheet1. If the 🧪 test row doesn't appear, 🔎 Debug in a tab opens the form pre-filled so you can press Submit and let Google tell you what it objects to — the invisible sends can't. SprayCast will never claim a row definitely arrived, because Google doesn't let the page find out.

9 · Backups, sharing a setup, and phones

  • 💾 Export backup — your only copy. There is no server: clearing this browser's site data takes everything with it. The file carries your locations, condition profiles, watches, scheduled sprays and your 📋 scorecard history — that last one especially, because nothing can rebuild it. The same file is how you hand your setup to a coworker; importing shows you exactly what's in it, and what it refused, before writing anything.
  • 🔗 Copy setup link — a link carrying your locations and condition profiles and nothing else: no scheduled sprays, no watches, no sheet connection, never a station token.
  • 🔔 Alerts chime while SprayCast is open in a tab. There is no server, so nothing can reach a closed tab or a sleeping phone. For a spray you must not miss, use 📅 Calendar on the scheduled spray.
  • 📱 On a phone — open the link, then "Add to Home Screen" and it runs like an app. It opens without signal on the last forecast it fetched, tells you the forecast is old rather than pretending otherwise, and refreshes when you're back online.
